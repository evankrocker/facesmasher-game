    Debt snowball calculator FAQ

    What is the debt snowball method?

    The debt snowball method is a debt payoff strategy where you list your debts from smallest balance to largest, pay the minimum on all of them, and put every extra dollar toward the smallest balance first. Once that debt is paid off, you roll its payment into the next-smallest debt, so your payment "snowballs" and grows as each debt disappears.

    Debt snowball vs. debt avalanche: what's the difference?

    The debt snowball method targets your smallest balance first, which builds momentum and quick wins. The debt avalanche method targets your highest interest rate first, which usually saves more money in total interest. This calculator uses the snowball method because the fast early payoffs tend to keep people motivated enough to finish.

    How is my debt-free date calculated?

    The calculator simulates your debts month by month: interest accrues on each balance, minimum payments are made on every debt, and any extra monthly payment is applied to your current target debt. As each debt is paid off, its minimum payment rolls into the next one. The date is simply today's date plus the number of months that simulation takes to reach a zero balance on every debt.

    Is my financial information stored or shared?

    No. Melt That Debt runs entirely in your browser with no backend server, database, or analytics. Nothing you type is ever transmitted or saved anywhere, and it disappears as soon as you close or refresh the tab.

    Can I use this calculator for credit cards, student loans, and medical bills?

    Yes. You can add up to 5 debts of any kind, including credit cards, personal loans, auto loans, student loans, and medical bills, as long as you know the balance, interest rate, and minimum monthly payment.
